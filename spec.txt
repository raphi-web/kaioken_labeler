This describes the wand as it is actually implemented, not as it was designed.
Where a decision looks arbitrary, the reason is given, because most of them were
settled by measurement against this project's own images and labels rather than
by preference.

Files:
  backend/api.py                          the whole selection algorithm
  backend/sam_service.py                  the SAM2 encoder the second source uses
  frontend/src/components/LabelerPane.jsx  tool state, preview, commit
  frontend/src/App.jsx                     the bridge call
  backend/test_wand.py                     30 invariant tests


================================================================================
1. WHAT IT DOES
================================================================================

Left-click an image region; the wand selects pixels of similar material around
that click and shows them as a pending selection. Enter paints the selection in
the active class; Escape discards it. Nothing touches the label buffer until
Enter.

Clicking again adds another sample: each click contributes its own region and
the results are unioned. Right-click marks material to EXCLUDE.

The essential property, and the one most of the design serves:

    ADDING A POSITIVE CLICK NEVER REMOVES A PIXEL.

Each sample is resolved completely independently — its own reference colour, its
own match set, its own connected region, its own pixel budget. Nothing about one
sample can reach another. A later click is therefore purely additive, which is
what makes the tool predictable enough to click repeatedly without watching your
selection shift under you.

(Negative clicks remove pixels by design. That is what they are for, and the red
marker is what distinguishes them.)

There are TWO SOURCES for what "similar material" means — the image's own bands,
or SAM2 encoder features (the `SAM features` switch). They share every line of
machinery below except the distance itself. See 2.2.


================================================================================
2. HOW A SELECTION IS COMPUTED
================================================================================

All of this runs in the backend, in Api.wand_select.

2.1 REFERENCE COLOUR (per sample)
    Api._sample_colour(y, x, sample_size)

    The mean spectrum of the `sample_size` pixels nearest the click, taken from
    a 5x5 window ordered nearest-first (_SAMPLE_OFFSETS). Only valid pixels
    contribute, so a click beside a nodata edge averages real ground rather than
    the fill value. The clicked pixel is always included.

    WHY AVERAGE: a one-pixel sample is a sample of size one, and on textured
    ground the clicked pixel is easily an outlier its own region fails to match.

    WHY NOT AVERAGE ACROSS SAMPLES: collapsing several clicks to a single
    centroid moves the reference for everything, so a new click could drop
    pixels an earlier one had selected — measured at 43 of 45 test regions.
    Averaging within one click denoises it without any such reach.

2.2 DISTANCE FIELD (per sample)
    Api._wand_fields(samples, sample_size, source, level)
        -> [((y, x), field), ...]

    One full-image field per sample: the distance from every pixel to that
    sample's reference. `source` picks how that distance is measured, and
    nothing else in this document changes with it — connectivity, the budget,
    the negatives and protection all consume only the field.

    CACHED per (source, level, sample point, sample_size) in Api._wand_cache,
    invalidated on image change and on a display-band change. Adding a click
    costs one new field; dragging the tolerance slider costs none; flipping
    between the two sources costs nothing after the first look at each.

2.2.1 SOURCE "image" — SPECTRAL (the default)
      Api._spectral_field(y, x, sample_size)

        distance = sqrt( mean over bands of (pixel_band - ref_band)^2 )

      Computed over EVERY band of SentinelImage.normalized, not the three-band
      RGB composite the pane displays. Water and terrain shadow are
      near-identical in true colour and separate cleanly in NIR/SWIR; the
      composite has discarded those bands before the frontend sees a pixel.

      Measured: all-band matching scores 0.524 mean IoU against ground truth
      versus 0.481 for RGB-only — +0.043, bootstrap confidence 93%. HSV was
      tried and scored WORSE than RGB (0.414); it re-encodes the same three
      numbers and a third of this imagery sits below saturation 0.15 where hue
      is noise.

      RMS per band rather than a plain Euclidean norm, so one tolerance value
      means the same thing whether a project has 4 bands or 12.

2.2.2 SOURCE "sam" — SAM2 ENCODER FEATURES
      Api._sam_features(level), Api._embedding_field(y, x, sample_size, level)

      The same click, asked a different question: not "which pixels are this
      colour" but "which pixels does SAM2's encoder describe the way it
      describes this one". Groups by texture and learned appearance rather than
      by reflectance.

        distance = 1 - cosine( pixel_feature, ref_feature )

      LEVEL selects one of the encoder's three outputs (sam_service.FEATURE_LEVELS):

        fine   high_res_feat0    32 ch at 256x256    DEFAULT
        mid    high_res_feat1    64 ch at 128x128
        deep   image_embed      256 ch at  64x64

      The grids are over the encoder's fixed 1024x1024 input frame, so on a
      512 px tile one `fine` cell is 2x2 px and one `deep` cell is 8x8 px. A
      pixel maps onto the grid by plain proportional scaling — the encoder
      resizes to a square regardless of aspect.

      FEATURES ARE CENTRED on the image's own mean feature before normalizing.
      This is load-bearing, not hygiene. Raw SAM2 features share a large common
      component, so everything is far from everything: measured on a 512 px
      tile, distances from a click to the rest of the scene ran 0.4 to 0.8, with
      the whole useful range inside the last tenth of that — at `mid`, tolerance
      0.5 selected 1.9% of the image and 0.7 selected 91.6%. Centred, the field
      starts at 0 at the click and spreads to ~1.5, and the tolerance has
      somewhere to work. The consequence is that a tolerance is relative to the
      variety in the tile in front of you, not to SAM2's feature space at large.

      COSINE, not a Euclidean distance: feature magnitude carries no meaning
      here and varies wildly between the three levels; the angle does. Same
      reason the spectral field uses an RMS.

      THE FIELD IS UPSAMPLED, NOT THE FEATURES. The distance is computed on the
      encoder's own grid and only the finished one-channel field is resized to
      image resolution (sam_service.resize_bilinear, matching how SAM2 upscales
      its own masks). Upsampling `deep`'s 256 channels to a 512 px image would
      cost 268 MB; its field costs 1 MB, and nothing downstream can tell — the
      flood fill, the valid mask and protection all still run at pixel
      resolution.

      A consequence worth knowing: after that upsample the exact zero at the
      reference cell survives nowhere (measured 0.09 at `fine` to 0.44 at
      `deep`, at the clicked pixel itself). The wand does not depend on it —
      2.3 seeds the sample into its own match set regardless.

      WHY `fine` IS THE DEFAULT, against the obvious measurement. Region growing
      needs a click's neighbours to resemble it, and by that test `fine` looks
      hopeless: the median distance within 10 px of a click is 1.10 against a
      whole-image median of 1.02 — the ground beside a click is as unlike it as
      the far side of the scene — while at `deep` the same numbers are 0.41
      against 0.98. But growing smoothly is not landing on the right thing. One
      `deep` cell covers 8x8 px, which at 10 m per pixel is 80 m of ground, and
      Sentinel-2 classes are often a few pixels across; its selections are the
      right shape at the wrong scale. `fine` grows grudgingly and needs most of
      the tolerance range, but its boundaries are where the material actually
      changes. Established by labeling with it, not by the metric.

      Likely mechanism: SAM2 learned its semantics on photographs, where a deep
      feature spans one object. A 10 m/px tile has nothing at that scale, so
      only the low-level texture features carry usable signal. If that is right,
      the ordering should flip on higher-resolution imagery — which is why all
      three levels stay selectable instead of the loser being deleted.

      COST: one encoder pass per image (~6 s on CPU), shared with the SAM2 snap
      tool through SamService's cache in both directions. Everything after is
      ~0.02 s. See 6.

      WHAT IT GIVES UP: the encoder only ever sees rgb_composite(), so this
      source discards exactly the extra bands 2.2.1 exists to use.

      IS IT WORTH IT: measured over 60 labeled regions, each source at its own
      best per-region tolerance — spectral 0.593 mean IoU, embedding 0.568,
      embedding winning 27 of 60, both clearing IoU>=0.5 on exactly 39. It is
      not a better tool. It is a differently-wrong one: the two IoUs correlate
      only 0.538, and picking the better per region would score 0.665. That is
      an oracle nobody has while labeling, but it is why the capability is kept
      as a switch on the wand rather than as a second tool or not at all.

2.3 MATCH SET (per sample)

        within = (field <= tolerance) AND valid_mask
        if negatives:  within &= (field < distance to nearest negative)
        within[sample] = True

    The sample joins its own match set whether or not it passes its own
    threshold, so a click is never a silent no-op.

    Nodata is excluded via valid_mask. (Only 14.tiff in this dataset has any:
    6,204 px.)

    The negative rule is nearest-neighbour: a pixel survives only while its own
    positive sample is a better match than every negative. One right-click on
    the material a fill is bleeding into therefore removes it WHEREVER IT
    APPEARS, not only where clicked — necessary because under global mode a
    bleed can be hundreds of scattered fragments.

2.4 REGION (per sample)

    Normal mode:  the 4-connected component of `within` containing the sample.
    Global mode:  all of `within`.

    4-CONNECTED, NOT 8: with 8 the fill crosses diagonals, so two regions
    touching at a single corner pixel count as one and the selection leaks
    through gaps too narrow to see at the zoom you clicked at.

    GLOBAL MODE exists because these classes are fragmented: one click reaches
    only 26–52% of a class, and Forest in 00.tiff is 63 disconnected pieces.

2.5 BUDGET (per sample)
    Api._grow_within_budget / Api._nearest_within_budget

    If a sample's region exceeds max_pixels, it is trimmed:

    Normal mode:  breadth-first growth from the sample, stopping at the budget.
                  Pixels arrive in order of distance THROUGH the region, so the
                  result is the innermost N and is always connected. Taking the
                  nearest N by straight-line distance would return two halves of
                  a horseshoe with the middle missing.
    Global mode:  the N pixels nearest the sample by straight-line distance.
                  With connectivity dropped there is no path to measure along.

    PER SAMPLE, NOT POOLED. A shared pot divides among the samples — measured:
    a second click left only 21% of what the first had selected. Worse than an
    even split, because one BFS queue hands pixels out in proportion to each
    region's frontier, so a fast-spreading region eats a slower one's allowance.

    Cost is O(samples * budget) and only runs when a region actually exceeds the
    cap; the connected-component pass is C and settles the common case alone.

2.6 UNION AND PROTECTION

    The per-sample regions are unioned. Then, if `protect` is set, pixels that
    already carry a label are dropped.

    APPLIED TO THE FINISHED SELECTION, NOT THE MATCH SET. A labeled pixel should
    still conduct the fill through itself — it simply does not get painted.
    Masking earlier would sever a region at the first labeled pixel it touches,
    so a fill could not reach unlabeled ground on the far side of a labeled strip.

    WHY IT EXISTS: this dataset is ~75% labeled and an unprotected fill covers
    35% already-labeled pixels across five classes. Overwriting finished work
    silently is the worse failure, so the safe mode is the one you switch off.

2.7 NOT IMPLEMENTED: HOLE FILLING

    Selections are speckled with interior holes — up to 281 holes / 29.5% of the
    filled area on 21.tiff. Closing them was considered and REJECTED.

    Those holes are real: a rock in a field, a tree in a meadow, pixels that
    genuinely differ from the class around them. Filling them would trade label
    accuracy for tidiness, which is the wrong direction for training data.


================================================================================
3. THE API
================================================================================

    Api.wand_select(samples, options) -> dict

Exposed to JS by pywebview as window.pywebview.api.wand_select. Runs on a
pywebview worker thread and returns JSON-serializable data.

INPUT
    samples   [[x, y], ...]  positive clicks, image pixel coordinates.
                             At least one is required.

    options   a single object rather than positional arguments, so a new knob
              is one key instead of another argument whose position nobody
              remembers:

      tolerance    float, REQUIRED. In the units of the chosen source: an RMS
                   per-band difference for "image" (typically 0.07–0.19), a
                   centred cosine distance for "sam" (typically 0.7–0.95 at the
                   fine level).
      source       "image" (default) or "sam". See 2.2.
      level        "fine" (default), "mid" or "deep". Ignored unless
                   source="sam".
      max_pixels   int or None (default None = unbounded). Budget PER SAMPLE.
      sample_size  int, default 1. Pixels averaged into each reference colour,
                   clamped to 1..MAX_SAMPLE_PIXELS (25). Under source="sam" it
                   counts FEATURE CELLS, not pixels.
      negatives    [[x, y], ...] or None. Exclusion clicks.
      global       bool, default false. Drop the connectivity bound.
      protect      bool, default false. Skip already-labeled pixels.
                   NOTE: the CALLER decides when this applies. It must NOT be
                   set while the eraser is active — the eraser only ever acts on
                   labeled pixels, so protecting them makes it a no-op.

OUTPUT (success)
      ok         true
      mask       base64 of np.packbits(mask) — the selection, one bit per
                 pixel, row-major. 32 KB for 512x512 rather than the 256 KB a
                 byte-per-pixel buffer would cost on every slider step.
      count      pixels in the returned mask (after budget and protection)
      available  pixels the selection would have had with no budget, so the UI
                 can say how hard the cap is biting
      protected  pixels withheld by `protect`
      capped     true if the budget trimmed any sample
      valid      count of non-nodata pixels in the image
      width      image width, needed to unpack the mask
      height     image height

OUTPUT (failure)
      ok: false, error: "<message>"

    Every bad input returns an error rather than raising: no image loaded,
    empty sample list, non-numeric tolerance / sample_size / max_pixels, a
    non-positive budget, an unknown source or level, SAM2 model files missing,
    and a failed encoder pass. The encode is forced BEFORE the sample loop so a
    model error comes back as a message the pane can show rather than a
    traceback across the bridge.

RELATED STATE
    Api._wand_cache = (image_name, {(source, level, y, x, sample_size): field})
        Cleared in Api._set_image, so a stale field can never survive an image
        switch, and in update_settings when the display bands change — the
        encoder reads those three bands, so its fields describe an image that no
        longer exists.
    Api._sam_stack_cache = (image_name, {level: centred unit-length features})
        Cleared alongside it. Holds the prepared stack, not the raw encoder
        output, so each new sample is one dot product.
    Api._labels
        Read by `protect`. Kept current by set_labels, which the frontend calls
        on every stroke end.

4. THE FRONTEND
================================================================================

4.1 BRIDGE
    App.jsx : handleWandSelect(samples, options)

    Calls api('wand_select', samples.map(p => [p.x, p.y]), options), then
    unpacks the bit-packed mask into a byte-per-pixel Uint8Array that the
    preview and the commit both consume. Source-agnostic: it forwards whatever
    options it is given.

    Passed down to LabelerPane as the onWandSelect prop.

4.2 TOOL STATE (LabelerPane.jsx)

    wandSamples      [{x, y, label}]  label 1 = include, 0 = exclude.
                                      Mirrored in wandSamplesRef for the
                                      window-level key handler.
    wandMaskRef      the current selection, byte per pixel
    wandTolerances   {classId: slider}, PER CLASS — only the values you have
                     changed; anything untouched falls through to that class's
                     measured seed (4.3)
    wandSam          bool, default false — the SAM-features switch
    wandSamRef       the same, for in-flight runs and effects
    wandSamTolerance slider 1..100, default 50; one value for every class
    wandLevel        'fine' | 'mid' | 'deep', default 'fine'
    wandBudgetStep   slider 1..100, default 100 (= no budget)
    wandSampleSize   slider 1..25,  default 9
    wandProtect      bool, default TRUE
    wandGlobal       bool, default false
    wandCount / wandCapped / wandProtected   readout values
    wandBusy         a request is in flight (cursor shows 'wait')
    wandSeq          sequence number; a response whose number is stale is
                     discarded, so dragging a slider cannot leave the preview
                     showing a value you have already moved past

    Changing the class, the source or the level RE-RUNS the pending samples
    rather than clearing them. For class and source that is a correctness
    requirement — the tolerance changes with both, so the preview would
    otherwise be the one the old threshold produced while the slider reads the
    new number, and Enter would commit a mask matching neither. For level it is
    the point of the selector: the three are only comparable on the same click.

4.3 SLIDER MAPPINGS

    TOLERANCE (source="image")
                backend tolerance = slider / WAND_TOL_SCALE (200).

                KEPT PER CLASS. What counts as "the same material" is a property
                of the material: the per-class optima span 0.07 to 0.19, and one
                global number is a compromise weighted toward whichever class
                you have most of. Seeds, in slider units:

                  Urban       38 (0.19)     Shrub        20 (0.10)
                  Raw-Earth   28 (0.14)     Meadow       18 (0.09)
                  Road        24 (0.12)     Agri-Green   18 (0.09)
                  Forest      22 (0.11)     Water        14 (0.07)
                  Wetland     22 (0.11)     (anything else)  26 (0.13)

                Keyed by class NAME, not id — these are properties of the
                material, and ids are per project. A renamed or unrecognised
                class falls back to 26.

                MEASURED over 388 regions across 20 images, clicking the most
                interior pixel of each connected labeled region and scoring IoU:
                seeded per class 0.397 mean IoU against 0.353 for a flat 0.13
                (+12.4%), share of regions reaching IoU>=0.5 30.9% -> 36.3%, and
                median over-selection halved, 10.6% -> 4.7%. 221 regions
                improve, 160 get worse, 7 unchanged — the mean rises because the
                wins are bigger, not because it helps everywhere. The ceiling if
                you retuned per region is 0.585.

                WHY 26 IS STILL THE FALLBACK: it cannot be improved on as a
                single number. The best fixed setting over those 388 regions is
                0.14 at 0.349 mean IoU against 0.347 for 0.13 — noise — and the
                curve is flat from 0.11 to 0.15. What pins it is the cliff to
                the right: median over-selection runs 6% at 0.13, 25% at 0.15,
                118% at 0.17 and 244% at 0.19 while IoU falls away slowly. The
                wand is additive, so selecting too little costs another click
                and selecting too much costs an undo and hand correction. The
                default belongs on the low side of the peak.

    TOLERANCE (source="sam")
                backend tolerance = 0.4 + (slider / 100) * 0.8, i.e. the slider
                spans [0.4, 1.2]. Default 50 = 0.8.

                NOT A BARE DIVIDE, because the useful distances do not start at
                zero. At `fine` on a 512 px tile, three clicks each selected
                under 0.15% of the image below 0.7, 0.6–2.3% at 0.8, and 7–30%
                by 1.0: a region at 0.7–0.95, a speck below, the scene above.
                A plain /100 left that whole band in the right-hand fifth of the
                travel. One value for every class: the per-class figures have
                not been measured for this source, and seeding it from the
                spectral ones would dress a guess in someone else's evidence.

    MAX PX      wandBudget(slider): log scale, 100 px .. 100,000 px, rounded to
                two significant figures; slider at max returns null (no cap).
                Log because useful budgets span three orders of magnitude — a
                road edge is a few hundred pixels, a field tens of thousands —
                and a linear slider would spend its travel where you never want.

    SAMPLE      passed through as-is, 1..25.
                Default 9 (a 3x3). Measured at a FIXED tolerance over 28
                regions, six clicks each: 1 -> 25 px raises click-to-click
                agreement 0.266 -> 0.288, cuts the spread in selected size from
                25.6x to 17.3x, and lifts accuracy 0.218 -> 0.233.
                NOTE the opposite result appears if tolerance is swept per
                click (1 px looks best) — a sweep can retune around a noisy
                reference after the fact, which nobody does while labeling.
                Inert for the tuning above: 0.343 to 0.349 mean IoU across
                1..25, so it is set from click-to-click agreement instead.

4.4 INTERACTION

    left click            add a positive sample, refill
    right click           add a negative sample, refill
    ctrl/alt + left       add a negative sample (trackpads that swallow
                          secondary click)
    Backspace             drop the most recent click of either kind
    Enter                 commit the selection to the active class
    Escape                discard the selection
    leaving wand mode     discards the pending selection

    The context menu is suppressed in wand mode so right-click is usable.

4.5 PREVIEW RENDERING
    LabelerPane.drawWandPreview(mask)

    Drawn on a dedicated canvas above the label canvas, pointer-events: none.

    FILL   the active class colour at WAND_PREVIEW_ALPHA (102 = 40%)
    EDGE   a two-tone boundary at WAND_EDGE_ALPHA (102 = 40%):
             - selected pixels missing a 4-neighbour  -> WAND_EDGE_LIGHT (255)
             - unselected pixels touching a selected  -> WAND_EDGE_DARK  (20)

    The edge is drawn AFTER the fill, so it sits on top rather than blending in.

    WHY AN EDGE AT ALL: the fill is in the class colour, so Snow (#eef2f7) over
    bright terrain, or Meadow over a yellow-green field, is nearly invisible.
    WHY TWO TONES: one always loses — a light outline vanishes on Snow, a dark
    one vanishes in shadow. The pair reads on both.

    Holes get outlined too, deliberately: see 2.7.
    Pixels at the image border count as edge, so a selection running off the
    edge still reads as bounded.

4.6 MARKERS
    An SVG overlay draws a dot per sample, pink for include and red for exclude
    — the same colours SAM2's click markers use in this pane, so the two
    click-driven tools share one language. The most recent marker is drawn
    brighter and thicker, so it is clear what Backspace will remove.

4.7 COMMIT
    LabelerPane.wandCommit()

    Writes the mask into the label buffer as ONE undoable diff
    (Map<pixelIndex, previousValue>), then clears the pending selection and
    calls onStrokeEnd, which pushes labels to the backend and schedules the
    autosave. Value written is the active class id, or UNLABELED when the
    eraser is active.

4.8 CONTROLS
    A dedicated row under the pane title, mounted only in wand mode: the three
    sliders on the left, then `SAM features`, the fine/mid/deep picker (mounted
    only while that switch is on, immediately beside it), `protect labels` and
    `whole image` on the right. The pane title bar already overflowed at three
    sliders.

    All three sliders respond to the scroll wheel. The tolerance readout shows
    the cosine value alongside the slider position under source="sam" — the band
    that works is narrow enough to be worth being able to name.

    The readout in the title bar shows sample count, selected pixels and
    percentage, and calls out `capped`, `protected`, and a runaway warning past
    WAND_WARN_FRACTION (40%) of the image.

    `SAM features` is disabled when sam2/onnx/ is missing, with the same tooltip
    the SAM2 tool button uses.

5. TESTS
================================================================================

    python3 backend/test_wand.py     (30 tests)

Runs against a scratch COPY of the project in /tmp, never label_raster/. An
earlier version wrote to the real ground truth and raced the running app's
autosave; do not reintroduce that.

SHARED BY BOTH SOURCES (24): the options contract and its error paths; strict
additivity of positive clicks across every tolerance/budget combination; a
sample's region being identical alone or alongside others; exact per-sample
budgets; connectivity after clipping; a sample always being inside its own
selection; nodata exclusion; count matching the mask; global mode's reach,
budget, nearest-first trimming and additivity; protection as a strict subset
that conducts through labeled pixels and reports what it withheld; negatives
shrinking the selection and reaching every fragment; and the combinations
(global+protect, negatives+global+protect+budget).

THE EMBEDDING SOURCE (6), skipped when sam2/onnx/ is absent:
  - a bad source or level is a message, not a traceback
  - the field is bounded in [0, 2], finite, and exactly the size of the image
    (it is upsampled from a smaller grid, so its shape is the one thing that
    could silently drift from the label buffer)
  - the two sources produce genuinely different fields — a guard against the
    embedding path quietly falling back to spectral
  - adding a sample never removes a pixel, on embeddings too
  - the same clicks give the same mask, the budget still bites, and a connected
    selection is still one piece
  - the shipped default tolerance lands inside the band where the source is
    usable at all (0.05%–25% of the image per click) — it fails if a re-exported
    encoder moves the distances, which is the point

The nodata test requests 14.tiff specifically — it is the only image in this
dataset with any nodata, and the test silently passed against 00.tiff before.

DELIBERATELY NOT TESTED: that the clicked pixel sits at distance zero under
source="sam". It does on the feature grid, by construction, but the field is
read at pixel resolution after a bilinear upsample, so the exact zero survives
nowhere (see 2.2.2). An earlier version of that test asserted it and failed
honestly.


================================================================================
6. PERFORMANCE
================================================================================

    first click, 1 sample                ~16 ms
    tolerance drag (cached fields)       ~1 ms per step
    3 samples, budget 3,200              ~20 ms
    3 samples, uncapped                  ~3 ms
    5 samples, budget 100,000            ~180 ms
    mask payload                         32 KB raw / 42.7 KB base64

    source="sam", first click on an image   ~6.0 s   (the encoder pass)
    source="sam", everything after          ~0.00–0.02 s
    sam_snap immediately after              ~0.04 s   (no re-encode)

    (512x512, 10 bands, on this machine. Re-measure rather than trust these if
    it matters — they were taken with a warm cache for everything but the
    first-click rows.)

The budget path is the expensive one, being O(samples * budget) Python. It runs
only when a region exceeds the cap. If a slider drag ever feels sticky, that is
where the time goes.

The encoder pass is the other one, and it is paid once per image and shared with
the SAM2 snap tool in both directions — whichever runs first pays for both. The
cursor shows 'wait' while it does.


================================================================================
7. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

- Tolerance is per class, but still global across the samples of one click set.
  Each sample has its own reference; they all share one threshold.

- A negative click can remove pixels a positive click had selected. By design,
  but it means the additivity guarantee is about positive clicks only.

- Global mode plus a loose tolerance can select most of the scene on the first
  click. The budget bounds it, but the budget defaults to unlimited. The 40%
  runaway warning is the only guard.

- Reference colours are per-image and per-session. Tuning a good Water sample
  on one image gives you nothing on the next; there is no stored per-class
  spectrum. This was discussed as "reusable class references" and not built.
  (The per-class TOLERANCE does persist for the session — the reference colour
  does not.)

- The per-class tolerance seeds are measured on this dataset's taxonomy and
  matched by name. Shrub (n=17), Water (n=18) and Wetland (n=4) rest on thin
  samples; 14 of the 34 images were not in the sweep.

- Under source="sam" the tolerance is relative to the variety in the current
  tile, because the features are centred per image. The same setting can bite
  differently on a uniform tile and a busy one.

- source="sam" sees only the RGB composite, discarding exactly the bands that
  justify computing the spectral field in the backend at all.
